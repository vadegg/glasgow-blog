## What the TRAPD survey translation method does

The TRAPD survey translation method helps preserve what a questionnaire is intended to measure when it moves between languages. A fluent translation can still alter a time frame, response option, level of formality or cultural assumption.

TRAPD means Translate, Review, Adjudicate, Pretest and Document. It is an iterative, team-based quality-assurance process: independent translations are compared, unresolved points are decided or deferred, intended respondents test the wording, and the team records decisions throughout. The [Cross-Cultural Survey Guidelines](https://ccsg.isr.umich.edu/chapters/translation/overview/) describe pretesting and documentation as integral to TRAPD.

This differs from relying on one forward translation or treating back translation as the main quality check. TRAPD concentrates on how target-language respondents understand and answer the instrument. Use it alongside broader [UX research methods](/blog/ux-research-methods/) when survey findings will inform product or service decisions.

## Prepare the source questionnaire and assign TRAPD roles

Start with a controlled source questionnaire. Assign a version number, freeze settled wording and flag items that remain open. This gives the team a reliable record of what was translated.

Write a translation brief covering the research objective, construct for each item, target population, country or region, language variety, reading level, survey mode, routing, response formats, and sensitive or technical terms. Survey Practice recommends briefing translation teams on the study, population, language, mode and questionnaire content ([Survey Practice](https://www.surveypractice.org/article/127842-development-of-a-practical-translation-approach-for-survey-research-projects)).

| Role | Responsibility | Decision right |
|---|---|---|
| Translators A and B | Create independent drafts and record alternatives or uncertainties | Recommend wording |
| Review moderator | Leads item-by-item comparison | Records consensus and open issues |
| Questionnaire or subject expert | Checks construct, terminology and survey design | Advises |
| Adjudicator | Resolves open issues and approves versions | Final decision |
| Pretest lead | Plans and synthesises testing | Recommends revisions |
| Documentation owner | Maintains the controlled record | Publishes approved record |

Collectively, the team needs target-language fluency, cultural knowledge, translation practice, questionnaire design, fieldwork knowledge and subject expertise. Reviewers can reconcile straightforward points; the adjudicator resolves what remains and authorises pretesting or fieldwork. The same ownership discipline appears in [survey design best practices](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/survey-design-best-practices-ux/): name the owner, record the rationale and test questions with intended respondents.

## Run the TRAPD translation workflow step by step

Treat the TRAPD translation workflow as a loop rather than five one-way stages.

1. **Translate independently.** Give each translator the source instrument, brief and terminology list. Ask them to record source-text problems and alternatives. Two versions help reveal meaning differences, idiosyncratic wording and oversights ([AAPOR and WAPOR](https://aapor.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/AAPOR-WAPOR-Task-Force-Report-on-Quality-in-Comparative-Surveys_Full-Report.pdf)).

2. **Review every respondent-facing element.** Compare introductions, instructions, questions, response scales, routing text, substitutions and interviewer notes. Assess wording in questionnaire context, not in isolation.

3. **Adjudicate open issues.** Judge alternatives against source intent, context and expert input. Approve wording, request further alternatives or defer the issue to pretesting. Document review questions that cannot be resolved and carry them into adjudication, as described in a practical TRAPD implementation ([Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1199989/full)).

4. **Pretest with intended respondents.** Test the approved version with people who match the field population and language variety. Return findings that affect meaning, answering or usability to the adjudicator. Retest consequential revisions.

5. **Document throughout.** Keep the source, drafts, reviewed wording, issue category, rationale, owner, status, pretest evidence and final wording in one controlled record. Documentation can also support later reuse of wording already translated, reviewed and tested ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/decoded/2022/04/22/how-we-translate-survey-questions-to-be-fielded-around-the-world/)).

`Adjudicate → pretest → revise → adjudicate → approve for fieldwork`

## Use a decision matrix to review and adjudicate each item

Use this worksheet as the item-level record for multilingual questionnaire translation. Preserve earlier versions rather than overwriting them.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q12 | Measure frequency during a rolling seven-day period in a neutral register | “During the last week, how often did you…?” | “In the previous week, how many times did you…?” | Time frame; response fit | “In the past seven days, how often did you…?” | Pretest revised wording | “Previous week” may suggest a calendar week; “how many times” may not fit a frequency scale | Illustrative cognitive-interview prompt: ask respondents to explain the period they used and select an answer on the intended scale | If respondents use a rolling period and the scale works, approve this wording; otherwise return to review. Adjudicator records the final decision. | 1.2 |

This is a hypothetical worked example, not a client case or reported pretest result. It shows the full hand-off from two drafts through review, adjudication, pretest evidence and final sign-off without claiming a universal outcome.

Review each item against these criteria:

| Criterion | Team question |
|---|---|
| Semantic meaning | Does it express the intended concept? |
| Completeness | Are qualifications and instructions retained? |
| Register | Is the formality suitable for respondents? |
| Cultural relevance | Does it depend on an unsuitable reference or assumption? |
| Response-scale equivalence | Can respondents use the available answers as intended? |
| Presentation | Does it work when spoken, displayed or read aloud? |
| Consistency | Does it match repeated terms elsewhere? |
| Survey logic | Do routing, validation and substitutions still work? |

## Pretest the culturally adapted questionnaire

Linguistic correctness does not establish that a question performs as intended. Pretest whether respondents understand the intended meaning, retrieve relevant information, make a judgement and map it to the available response options.

Recruit people who reflect relevant language varieties and field-population characteristics. Consider literacy, location, migration history or professional role where these affect language use. Cognitive interviewing can examine comprehension, recall, judgement, response mapping and sensitivity. Add mode testing where interviewer delivery, mobile layout, visual scales or routing may alter the response process. See [cognitive interviewing for survey pretesting](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/cognitive-interviewing-survey-pretesting/) for a practical interview structure.

Set issue-based revision criteria rather than using an assumed universal sample size. Escalate items where findings suggest changed meaning, inconsistent interpretation, failed routing or an unusable response scale. For each material issue, record the adjudicator’s disposition: revise, reject, investigate further or accept a documented risk.

## Document decisions and control multilingual versions

Documentation is working quality control, not retrospective paperwork. Maintain a decision log, terminology list and version register.

| Record | Minimum contents |
|---|---|
| Decision log | Item ID, source intent, candidate translations, issue, owner, rationale, pretest evidence, final wording, status and version |
| Terminology list | Approved term, definition, language equivalent, usage notes, owner and approval date |
| Version register | Instrument version, language, change summary, approval status, approver and release date |

Keep stable item IDs across languages and preserve superseded wording. Reuse approved wording cautiously: review it again when the population, mode, context or source item changes.

Before release, confirm that every open query has an explicit disposition; programmed text matches the approved version; scales, routing and substitutions work; comparable concepts are harmonised where required; the adjudicator has signed off; and records are archived. Good [research operations](/blog/research-operations/) make these controls manageable across languages and waves.

## Adapt the workflow without calling a shortcut full TRAPD

Parallel translation and pretesting require time, specialist access and coordination. Survey Practice characterises TRAPD as resource- and time-intensive, while recognising less intensive variations.

If resources are constrained, prioritise full review and pretesting for new, sensitive, high-impact or culturally difficult items. Label a reduced process accurately, record omitted stages and state the remaining risk. Expert confidence does not replace evidence from intended respondents.

Start with a source freeze and a clear brief. Name owners, translate independently, review, adjudicate, pretest, revise and release only the approved version.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does TRAPD stand for in survey translation?

Translate, Review, Adjudicate, Pretest and Document. Documentation spans every stage, and pretest findings can return an item to adjudication.

### How many translators does the TRAPD method use?

The model uses two or more independent versions. Teams may use full parallel translation or overlapping split translation, but a single translation gives the team less direct comparison.

### Is TRAPD the same as back translation?

No. TRAPD combines independent forward translations, multidisciplinary review, adjudication and respondent pretesting. Back translation converts target wording back into the source language as a separate check.

### Who should adjudicate a translated questionnaire?

Choose someone with authority to make the final decision and sufficient knowledge of the research design, questionnaire topic and translation project. If roles are combined on a small project, record that arrangement.

### What should a TRAPD decision log contain?

Include the item ID, source intent, candidate translations, issue type, reviewed wording, unresolved query, decision owner, rationale, pretest evidence, final wording, status and version.

### Can a small commercial research team use TRAPD?

Yes. Scope languages and high-risk items carefully, preserve independent input where possible, and disclose reduced coverage or omitted pretesting.
