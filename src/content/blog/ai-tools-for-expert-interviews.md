# AI Tools for Expert Interviews: A Practical Guide for B2B Teams

In unfamiliar or complex B2B markets, expert interviews are a critical research method to gain nuanced insights that customer interviews often miss. Unlike customer interviews, which focus on end-user experience, expert interviews tap into deep domain knowledge, competitive intelligence, and industry trends. Recent advances in AI have transformed how these interviews are sourced, conducted, and analyzed, making the process faster and more scalable. But not all AI tools are created equal. Understanding their capabilities, trade-offs, and costs is essential to selecting the right solution for your team.

## When to Choose Expert Interviews Over Customer Interviews

Customer interviews excel at validating product-market fit and user experience assumptions. However, when entering a new vertical, assessing complex technologies, or needing strategic foresight, expert interviews are indispensable. Experts provide context on market dynamics, regulatory environments, and competitor strategies that customers may not fully grasp or articulate. For B2B SaaS teams and agencies, expert interviews can uncover hidden risks and opportunities that shape product roadmaps and go-to-market plans.

## AI-Native vs Legacy Expert Networks: What’s the Difference?

Legacy expert networks like GLG and AlphaSights rely heavily on human curators and established expert pools. They offer broad coverage and compliance rigor but come with high costs and slower turnaround times. AI-native platforms such as Techspert and Infoquest leverage machine learning for expert matching and workflow automation, reducing friction and price points. However, these newer platforms may have narrower domain coverage and evolving compliance frameworks.

- **Pricing:** Legacy networks often charge premium rates with minimum engagement fees, while AI-native tools offer subscription or pay-per-use models with more transparent pricing.  
- **Compliance:** Legacy providers have mature compliance processes suited for regulated industries such as finance and healthcare. AI-native tools are catching up but may require additional due diligence.  
- **Accessibility:** AI-native platforms provide self-serve interfaces enabling smaller teams to access expert interviews without broker dependency.

## AI Features That Accelerate Insight Generation

- **Expert Matching:** AI algorithms analyze profiles, publications, and past engagements to recommend the best-fit experts within minutes, reducing manual search time by up to 70%.  
- **Scheduling Automation:** Integrated calendars and automated reminders streamline logistics, cutting coordination time by half.  
- **Transcription and Summarization:** Real-time transcription paired with natural language processing extracts key themes and quotes, speeding up analysis.  
- **Thematic Insight Extraction:** AI tools cluster insights across interviews to identify patterns and emerging trends, supporting faster decision-making.

## Risks and Limitations to Consider

- **Expert Attrition:** AI platforms depend on active expert pools. Niche domains may face scarcity or turnover, impacting availability.  
- **Platform Maturity:** Newer AI-native tools may lack robust support or integration capabilities compared to legacy networks.  
- **Geographic and Domain Coverage:** Some platforms excel in tech and finance but underperform in regulated or emerging markets.  
- **AI Limitations:** Automated insights require human validation to avoid misinterpretation or bias.

## Decision Framework: Selecting the Right Tool

Consider these criteria to align tool choice with your team’s needs:

- **Team Size:** Larger teams may benefit from legacy networks’ scale; smaller teams gain agility with AI-native tools.  
- **Budget:** Legacy networks command higher fees; AI-native platforms offer flexible pricing.  
- **Domain Complexity:** Highly regulated or niche domains may require legacy providers’ compliance and expert depth.  
- **Speed and Scale:** AI-native tools excel at rapid expert matching and scheduling automation.  
- **Compliance Needs:** Evaluate platform certifications and data handling policies carefully.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

AI tools for expert interviews have lowered barriers to accessing specialized knowledge, but choosing the right platform requires balancing cost, coverage, compliance, and AI capabilities. Expert interviews are not a wholesale replacement for customer interviews but a complementary approach when domain expertise is critical.
