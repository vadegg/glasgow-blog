Product research gathers evidence for decisions about a product: whose problem to solve, which alternatives matter, what to build, how people use it and what makes adoption possible. In B2B SaaS, the user, administrator and buyer may be different people. Research has to account for those differences.

## Start with the decision you cannot make confidently

| Decision | Evidence needed | Start here |
|---|---|---|
| Which audience to serve | Context, needs, alternatives and a workable route to reach people | [Market segmentation](/blog/market-segmentation-what-it-helps-you-decide-and-what-it-does-not/) |
| Why someone would switch | Recent buying or switching events and constraints | [JTBD interviews](/blog/jtbd-interviews-b2b-saas/) |
| What prevents a sale | Buyer criteria, approval steps and rejected alternatives | [B2B buyer research](/blog/b2b-buyer-research-methods/) |
| Whether a flow is usable | Observed task performance and recoverability | [UX research methods](/blog/ux-research-methods/) |
| Which concept to investigate | Understanding, relevance and unresolved assumptions | [Concept testing](/blog/concept-testing/) |
| Why customers leave | Recent cancellation events, usage context and constraints | [Churn research](/blog/churn-research-for-saas/) |
| Which market claims are credible | Defined market boundaries and traceable sources | [B2B market research](/blog/b2b-market-research/) |

Choose a narrow decision that has an owner and a consequence. “Understand customers better” is a direction; “decide whether setup needs an administrator handoff” is a researchable problem. The [GOV.UK service research planning guide](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service) is a useful reference for organising a programme around what a team needs to learn.

## Use a one-page research brief

Copy this outline into the [research plan template](/blog/user-research-plan-template/):

1. **Decision:** what will be chosen, by whom and when?
2. **Uncertainty:** what could make the current proposal wrong?
3. **People and context:** who experiences, influences or authorises the relevant action?
4. **Existing evidence:** what do support, analytics, sales and prior research already show?
5. **Missing evidence:** which observation or conversation could change the decision?
6. **Limits:** which groups, markets or situations will the study not cover?
7. **Output:** which working document will the decision owner use?
8. **Follow-up:** what will be checked after a decision is made?

For example, a hypothetical team considering an enterprise tier may need buyer interviews and delivery-cost estimates before another interface test. Usability cannot establish a procurement path; a buyer's enthusiasm cannot establish task success.

## Keep customer, market and product evidence distinct

[Customer research](/blog/customer-research/) examines people's circumstances and experience. [Market research methods](/blog/market-research-methods-which-method-fits-which-decision/) investigate demand, alternatives and the wider commercial setting. Product research connects the appropriate evidence to a product choice.

Competitor features are hypotheses about useful capabilities, not proof that your customers need them. Use [competitor analysis](/blog/competitor-analysis-without-copying-the-wrong-competitors/) to test assumptions about alternatives rather than copy a roadmap.

## Use AI with an inspectable evidence trail

AI tools can support collection and analysis, but a fluent summary is not a source. Keep findings linked to observations, verify consequential claims and make uncertainty visible. Start with [AI tools for product research](/blog/ai-tools-for-product-research/) for the landscape and [tool evaluation criteria](/blog/how-to-choose-ai-user-research-tools/) for a purchasing decision.

## Turn the study into an accountable decision

Assign ownership through [research operations](/blog/research-operations/). Use [product discovery](/blog/product-discovery/) for risky assumptions and [insight to impact](/blog/insight-to-impact/) to record what changed. Measure outcomes separately from the number of interviews or pages in a report.
