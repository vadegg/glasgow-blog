## How much should you pay user research participants?

There is no defensible universal UK rate for user research participant incentives. Set an amount from the method, total participant effort, direct expenses, audience scarcity, scheduling burden and recruitment urgency.

Pay for time and contribution separately from travel, care, accessibility support and other direct costs. The [Department for Education](https://user-research.education.gov.uk/guidance/incentives-26/5) says there is no standard monetary incentive value. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/find-user-research-participants) says the amount depends on participant type and session length.

Use the worksheet below to approve a documented amount, payment plan and review date. It gives your team a decision trail rather than a generic figure copied from another study. For wider planning, see our [research operations guidance](/blog/research-operations/).

## Start with method and actual participant time

Classify the study before discussing payment. Count real participant effort, not only the calendar slot.

| Method | Include in participant effort |
| --- | --- |
| Self-administered survey | Reading, consent and completion |
| Unmoderated task or recorded test | Setup, installation, tasks and upload |
| Moderated remote interview or usability session | Preparation, consent, session and follow-up |
| In-person session | Session activity, travel and waiting |
| Diary or longitudinal study | Entries, prompts, check-ins and final discussion |
| Group session | Preparation, travel, waiting and group activity |

Include preparation, consent, installation, travel, waiting, tasks and follow-up. For multi-part work, calculate each activity and plan pro-rata payment where appropriate. The [Health Research Authority](https://www.hra.nhs.uk/about-us/committees-and-services/nreap/payments-and-incentives-research/) says pro-rata payments should always be offered in its health-research guidance.

For self-administered studies on its platform, [Prolific](https://participant-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445043-underpaying-studies) requires at least £6 per hour and recommends £9 per hour or more. This is a platform benchmark, not evidence of a standard UK rate for moderated interviews or usability sessions.

Choose the method that answers the research question before pricing it. Our guide can help you [choose the right UX research method](/blog/ux-research-methods/), including where [AI-moderated interviews](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/ai-moderated-interviews-when-how-to-run/) fit.

## Adjust the amount for audience scarcity, not assumptions

Record the answers to four questions before approving an adjustment:

1. How narrowly is the audience screened?
2. Is participation during paid working time?
3. Does the person bring specialist expertise or seniority?
4. How inconvenient are the scheduling requirements or format?

B2B research participant incentives should reflect the individual’s time and the practical cost of taking part. A senior title alone is not a rationale for an arbitrary premium. The [Market Research Society](https://www.mrs.org.uk/pdf/3.%20MRS%20Guidelines%20for%20Business%20to%20Business%20Research,%20Insight%20and.pdf) says monetary incentives should be reasonable and appropriate to the participant and project. Where suitable, a findings summary can complement payment.

Do not pay someone more or less for the same contribution because they are disabled. Budget separately for the support that enables participation, such as communication support, transport, a remote option or a different location.

Before repeatedly raising the incentive, test whether payment is the actual barrier. Widen genuinely non-essential criteria, extend fieldwork, offer more time slots or change an unsuitable format. Keep qualification standards clear with [UX research screener questions and branching logic](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/screener-survey-best-practices-ux-research/) and processes to [detect fake and AI-generated research participants](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/detect-fake-ai-generated-participants-user-research/).

## Budget access, travel and recruitment separately

The payment reaching a participant is only one programme cost. Record each line separately:

- Incentive for participation
- Participant travel and direct expenses
- Accessibility support
- Recruiter or panel fees
- Payment-processing fees
- No-show contingency
- Total programme cost

For in-person research, keep travel separate from the base incentive. The Department for Education says travel costs must be covered for in-person participation, while GOV.UK notes that access-related expenses may be additional. Separate figures make remote and in-person studies easier to compare and avoid asking participants to absorb costs.

Ask disabled participants what support they need rather than guessing. Read more about how to [recruit disabled participants for accessibility research](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/recruit-disabled-participants-accessibility-research/).

Direct recruitment and panel recruitment can produce different operational costs even where the participant payment is the same. Take a full-cost view when estimating [how much user research costs](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/how-much-does-user-research-cost/).

## Make payment fair, clear and compliant

Before launch, tell participants what will be paid, who will administer it, when it will be made, which payment method will be used and any clearly disclosed quality-control conditions.

For live sessions, pay at the beginning where practical. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/find-user-research-participants) recommends this so payment does not appear to depend on particular answers. It must not depend on favourable feedback.

Check the payment method against organisational policy and the [MRS B2B guidelines](https://www.mrs.org.uk/pdf/3.%20MRS%20Guidelines%20for%20Business%20to%20Business%20Research,%20Insight%20and.pdf). Payments above reasonable participation expenses can have tax implications for an individual. [HMRC guidance](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/employment-income-manual/eim71105) says an excess may be taxable as miscellaneous income in relevant circumstances. Do not give personal tax advice.

Pause for legal, ethics or safeguarding review for clinical research, children, people unable to consent, highly sensitive topics, or possible benefits and employment implications. If sessions are recorded, align payment wording with your [AI notetaker consent and privacy](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/ai-notetaker-consent-privacy-user-research/) process.

## Use the UK Incentive Approval Worksheet

Copy this worksheet into the study brief.

1. **Define study and audience:** Record method, purpose, eligibility criteria and recruitment channel.
2. **Calculate participant effort:** List preparation, consent, tasks, session, travel, waiting and follow-up.
3. **Add direct expenses:** Record travel, care, accessibility support and other agreed costs separately.
4. **Record adjustments:** Note scarcity, seniority, working-time disruption, scheduling burden and urgency.
5. **Choose payment:** State the amount, method, administrator, timing and participant wording.
6. **Check ethics and compliance:** Confirm policy, tax-information wording, consent, safeguarding and required review.
7. **Price operations:** Add recruiter, panel, processing and no-show costs.
8. **Set a checkpoint:** Review qualified applications, show rates and no-shows after five working days.

**Illustrative fictional example**

- Study: Moderated remote usability session with a narrowly screened professional audience.
- Participant effort: Consent, setup, session and follow-up recorded in the study brief.
- Incentive: £[organisation-approved amount].
- Direct expenses: £0 expected; accessibility support budgeted separately if requested.
- Scarcity notes: Specialist workflow experience required; evening slots available to reduce work disruption.
- Payment: Digital payment sent by the named administrator at session start where practical.
- Ethics and compliance: Payment wording, recording consent and any policy review completed before launch.
- Operational costs: Recruiter fee, payment fee and no-show contingency recorded separately.
- Review date: Fifth working day after launch.

At review, inspect qualified applications, show rates and no-shows. If qualified applications are arriving and appointments are filling, hold the incentive. If they are not, check reach, screener friction, accessibility and scheduling before raising payment. Increase it only when those checks indicate that the approved amount remains the credible barrier.

A maintained panel can make these signals easier to read. See how to [build and maintain a UX research panel](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/ux-research-panel-management/) and [recruit participants for user research](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/how-to-recruit-participants-for-user-research/). A global recommendation calculator such as [User Interviews’ incentive calculator](https://www.userinterviews.com/blog/the-user-research-incentive-calculator) serves a different purpose: this worksheet records the UK-specific decision and review trail for one study.

## Common incentive mistakes to avoid

Do not publish one amount as a universal UK standard. A suitable rate for a short self-administered study does not determine payment for specialist B2B interviews, diary studies or in-person sessions.

Keep reimbursement, participant incentives and supplier fees as separate figures. Do not make payment contingent on positive feedback, undisclosed conditions or full completion where pro-rata payment is appropriate.

Do not keep increasing payment when an over-restrictive screener, short fieldwork window or inaccessible logistics is blocking recruitment. Document the rationale, test recruitment for five working days and revise the plan using qualified fill signals.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a fair UK user research incentive?

A fair amount reflects participant time, burden, direct costs and the difficulty of reaching the required audience. Use the worksheet rather than a generic market number. Prolific’s £6 per hour minimum and £9 per hour recommendation apply to self-administered studies on that platform, not all UK research.

### Should B2B research participants be paid more?

Specialist access, seniority, work disruption and scarcity may justify a higher amount, but no job-title-only rate exists. The MRS says sharing relevant findings can be an appropriate B2B incentive alongside reasonable payment.

### Should travel expenses be included in the incentive?

No. Document and budget direct travel and access costs separately so the base incentive remains comparable and participation does not depend on absorbing those costs.

### When should research incentives be paid?

State the amount, administrator, method and timing in advance. For live sessions, GOV.UK recommends giving the incentive at the start where practical, so participants do not perceive it as dependent on their answers.
