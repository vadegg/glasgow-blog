## Start with the participant, not the recruitment channel

You can recruit B2B research participants without a panel. You cannot skip a precise participant definition and controlled screening.

Start with the decision the research must inform. Define must-have criteria: current responsibilities, decision role, relevant behaviour, company type, market and recency of experience. A job title alone is not evidence of eligibility. For research on finance-system selection, ask whether the person recently selected, renewed or administered a system.

Keep essential criteria separate from quotas. Essentials protect the research question; quotas prevent one group dominating the sample. Before outreach, define exclusions, accessibility needs, session format, incentive approach and target completes. Build [screener questions and branching logic](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/screener-survey-best-practices-ux-research/) around actions and responsibilities rather than claimed interest.

This is part of sound [research operations](/blog/research-operations/). Give invitees enough information to decide whether to take part, without revealing detail that could bias their answers ([UK Home Office](https://design.homeoffice.gov.uk/user-research/participant-recruitment)).

## Compare the four panel-free recruitment routes

How to recruit B2B research participants without a panel depends on the audience, not a universal channel ranking. Speed, cost, response and verification effort vary with seniority, geography, audience incidence, sample size and the relationships available to your team. Use more than one route where a single source could skew the sample; Government Digital Service guidance recommends varying recruitment approaches to limit this risk ([Government Digital Service](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/find-user-research-participants)).

| Route | Suitable audience | Access beyond customers | Internal effort | Control and verification | Data handoff and bias risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer or prospect lists | Users, buyers and lost opportunities | Limited to records you hold | Medium | High control over records and screening | Can over-represent active or favourable contacts |
| Professional networks and communities | Defined professional groups | Good where relevant groups exist | Medium | Researchers review candidates directly | Community membership may shape the sample |
| Targeted cold outreach | Narrow roles, firms or markets | High | High | Full control, but substantial screening work | Lower response may leave gaps in quotas |
| Specialist recruitment agency | Senior, rare or urgent audiences | High | Lower sourcing effort | Requires oversight of screener and sourcing | Quality depends on supplier practice and data handling |

Use [general participant recruitment methods](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/how-to-recruit-participants-for-user-research/) as the foundation, then choose a mix that preserves the comparisons the study needs. Direct profile sourcing and outreach are valid options, but not the only routes available ([Koji](https://www.koji.so/blog/b2b-participant-recruitment-without-a-panel)).

**Hypothetical route-selection matrix: 10 UK mid-market finance-system decision-makers**

| Route | Target completes | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Customer and prospect records | 3 | Include current users and recently lost opportunities |
| Professional communities and warm introductions | 2 | Reach relevant peers outside the account base |
| Targeted cold outreach | 3 | Fill company-size and sector gaps |
| Specialist recruiter | 2 | Provide contingency for senior or difficult-to-book participants |

This allocation stops current customers defining the whole sample and gives the team an alternative route if another underperforms.

## Use customer and prospect lists without sampling only advocates

CRM, support, product-use, sales and churn records may identify people with relevant buying, implementation or service experience. They can also produce a narrow sample if account owners nominate only cooperative contacts.

Segment deliberately. Active customers, new users, low-engagement accounts, lost opportunities and former customers may bring different perspectives. Ask account owners to facilitate access, but keep the screener and final selection independent.

Participation must be voluntary. Do not imply that taking part will affect service, renewal or account status. Before exporting or contacting people, confirm that the planned use of their details is appropriate and that the data has been obtained and managed compliantly. Refer uncertain cases to privacy or legal review ([Department for Education](https://user-research.education.gov.uk/guidance/recruiting-participants/self-recruitment)). Record each participant’s source and segment so findings can be interpreted in light of the sample.

## Recruit through professional networks and targeted outreach

Warm introductions, community posts, public calls and one-to-one cold messages need different handling. For community recruitment, obtain moderator permission. Say who is conducting the research, what it broadly covers, the expected time, format and any incentive. Do not present research as networking, advice or a sales conversation.

For direct sourcing, build a broad list from observable signals such as role, employer, sector and location. These signals justify screening; they do not prove eligibility. Use neutral questions to confirm recent responsibilities.

A concise invitation should cover:

- Your identity and organisation
- Why the person may be relevant
- A non-leading description of the study
- Time commitment and format
- Incentive, if offered
- Privacy information and an easy decline or opt-out route

Pilot a small batch before scaling. Track delivery, response, qualification and booking outcomes by route instead of using a generic response-rate benchmark. Public contact details do not remove data-protection obligations: processing personal data needs a lawful basis ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/the-research-provisions/principles-and-grounds-for-processing/)). Plan adjustments using [inclusive recruitment for disabled participants](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/recruit-disabled-participants-accessibility-research/).

## Know when to hire a B2B participant recruitment agency

A specialist recruiter is useful when the audience is rare, senior, geographically dispersed, outside your network or beyond internal capacity. The supplier can take on sourcing work, but your team remains accountable for sample quality.

Ask how the agency sources participants and whether it uses fresh recruitment or an existing database. Review its sector experience, identity and experience checks, replacement process, accessibility support, incentive handling and privacy responsibilities.

Send a written brief covering target completes, must-haves, quotas, exclusions, timings, session format and incentive assumptions. Government Digital Service guidance says to provide a written brief even where an agency accepts verbal instructions ([Government Digital Service](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/write-a-recruitment-brief)). Review the screener and proposed candidates yourself. Compare total delivered cost, internal time, replacement terms and qualification quality, rather than headline price alone. A [UK participant incentive framework](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/user-research-participant-incentives-uk/) can help assess incentives against participant burden.

## Screen and verify B2B participants consistently

Apply the same core eligibility rules across every route. A warm introduction and an agency booking do not prove that someone qualifies.

Ask about actions before revealing what qualifies. For example: “What part did you play in the last finance-system review?” Follow with proportionate checks of role, employer and relevant experience. A work email, company website or public professional profile may support a claim; collect only information needed to manage participation. Government guidance advises collecting and using the minimum necessary information ([Government Digital Service](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/managing-user-research-data-participant-privacy)).

Eligibility is not consent. Explain how research data will be used, then obtain informed consent before recording or using session data.

Set these controls before outreach:

- Duplicate contacts and previous participation
- Conflicts of interest
- Frequent professional participants
- Inconsistent or suspicious responses
- Accessibility requirements and adjustments
- Withdrawal, replacement and deletion procedures

Where the risk warrants it, use guidance on how to [detect fake and AI-generated participants](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/detect-fake-ai-generated-participants-user-research/) and [detect bots and deepfakes in live interviews](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/detect-bots-deepfakes-research-participant-interviews/).

## Build a launch-ready recruitment worksheet

Use this completed, hypothetical worksheet as a starting point for a 10-interview recruit.

| Field | Filled example |
|---|---|
| Research decision | Understand how UK mid-market firms select finance systems |
| Target completes | 10 interviews |
| Must-haves | Current decision role; 100–999 employees; involved in selection, renewal or implementation in the past 12 months |
| Quotas | At least 3 customers or prospects; at least 5 non-customers; no more than 4 from one sector |
| Exclusions | Vendors, consultants selling finance systems and direct competitors |
| Route allocation | 3 customer/prospect records; 2 communities or introductions; 3 cold outreach; 2 agency contingency |
| List owner / screener owner | CRM owner / research lead |
| Verification checks | Behavioural screener; role and employer check; duplicate and conflict review |
| Incentive and privacy review | Agreed before outreach / review completed |
| Dates | Pilot first; set later outreach and session dates from observed outcomes |
| Escalation triggers | Add or switch a route if eligible candidates miss the booking milestone; pause a route if one segment exceeds quota; investigate repeated verification failures; increase confirmed bookings if attendance threatens session dates |

After the pilot, calculate remaining outreach from observed contact, response, qualification, booking and attendance rates. Adjust outreach or the channel mix before weakening a criterion essential to the research question.

## Turn one-off recruitment into reusable research operations

Keep route-level figures for qualified candidates, bookings, attendance, replacements and represented segments. Retain process evidence without keeping personal data beyond the approved purpose.

Ask separately for permission to contact someone about future studies. Consent to one interview is not permanent panel consent. If you recruit the same audience repeatedly and can support the governance and maintenance, an owned pool may be appropriate. Occasional specialist studies may still need external sourcing. Learn how to [build and manage a user research panel](https://blog.glasgow.works/blog/build-manage-user-research-panel/), then use your recruitment evidence to decide.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you recruit B2B research participants with no customers?

Yes. Use professional communities, warm introductions, targeted cold sourcing or specialist agencies. Choose based on audience incidence, timeline, verification burden and internal capacity.

### How many people should you contact for B2B interviews?

There is no reliable universal ratio. Pilot each route and measure contact, qualification, booking and attendance. Use those results to calculate the outreach still required.

### Should B2B research participants be paid?

Consider seniority, time commitment, inconvenience, accessibility costs, organisational restrictions and ethical risk. Incentives may improve access to difficult groups, but teams should consider and mitigate the associated risks ([Department for Education](https://user-research.education.gov.uk/guidance/incentives-26/3)).

### When should you use a B2B participant recruitment agency?

Use one for specialised, external, time-sensitive or operationally demanding audiences. Assess sourcing transparency, verification, replacements, privacy responsibilities and total delivered cost.

### How do you verify that a B2B participant is qualified?

Combine neutral behavioural screening with proportionate checks of current role, employer and relevant experience. Apply the same rules across channels and collect only the data needed for eligibility and administration.

Start with the worksheet, run a small pilot and expand the routes producing the sample your study needs.
