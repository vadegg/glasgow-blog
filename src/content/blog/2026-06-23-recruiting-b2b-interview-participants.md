B2B interview recruitment starts with the decision your study must inform. Specify the relevant work or purchase event, identify the people who took part, then choose channels that can reach them. A job title alone does not establish that someone can answer your research question.

This guide includes an adaptable recruitment plan and invitation. Its examples are hypothetical; they are not reported campaign results or industry response-rate benchmarks.

## Define the event and role you need to understand

For a study about adopting invoice software, “finance leaders” is too broad. You may need people who recently evaluated suppliers, approved a budget, reviewed security or handled invoice exceptions. One person can hold several roles; some organisations divide them across teams.

Write the event, recency window and role required for each research question. Explain why each restriction is necessary. Do not add company-size, industry or seniority filters simply because they are available in a panel dashboard. Nielsen Norman Group's [participant selection guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/selection-criteria/) supports choosing criteria around the behaviour under investigation.

Use the [buying-committee canvas](/blog/b2b-buyer-research-methods/) when the question concerns a purchase, and the [screener worksheet](/blog/screener-survey-best-practices-ux-research/) to turn criteria into neutral questions and branching decisions.

## Match the channel to the missing perspective

| Channel | Useful access | Sampling risk to check |
|---|---|---|
| Customer contacts or an opt-in panel | People with known product experience | Happy or engaged accounts may dominate |
| Customer-success introductions | People who can recognise the research organisation | Colleagues may nominate only easy contacts |
| Churned accounts and lost prospects | Experiences excluded from an active-customer list | Reachability and willingness can differ |
| Professional communities and associations | Relevant specialist work | Community members may differ from the wider audience |
| Specialist recruiters or research panels | Roles absent from your existing network | Claimed role, recent behaviour and verification still need checking |

These are tradeoffs to assess, not a ranking of channel speed or quality. Keep the original channel on each participant record so you can inspect who is missing from the final sample. The GOV.UK service manual describes [several participant recruitment routes](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/find-user-research-participants); choose according to the people your study needs.

For a retention question, include the relevant [churn research](/blog/churn-research-for-saas/) perspective. For a purchase question, use [win–loss research](/blog/win-loss-analysis-b2b-saas/) to distinguish recent buying outcomes. Neither mix is mandatory for every study.

## Send an invitation people can evaluate

State the organisation, broad research purpose, format, duration, date range and proposed compensation. Explain whether the conversation will be recorded and where fuller participation information is available. Invite questions and make declining straightforward.

**Hypothetical invitation — replace the bracketed fields:**

> Hello [name], I am [researcher] at [organisation]. We are studying how people handle [broad workflow], and would like to invite you to a [duration] conversation during [date range]. This is research, with no sales presentation. Participation is optional. We offer [compensation, if applicable], subject to any employer restrictions. [Participation information link.] If you are interested, you can [reply or choose a time]. Please let me know if you would prefer no further contact.

Do not disclose every qualifying answer in the invitation. Keep a broad relevance statement there and assess detailed eligibility in the screener. A reply and a booking link are both reasonable next steps; measure which process works for your audience instead of treating one as a universal conversion rule.

## Screen, schedule and confirm participation

Ask about a recent concrete event: what happened, what the person did and which part they owned. Follow up when a response is ambiguous. Assess unfamiliar terminology in context; polished wording or speed alone does not prove authenticity or fraud.

Before the session, confirm time zone, accessibility needs, meeting access and the participation information. A participant may need internal permission to discuss work or receive compensation. A donation is not automatically an acceptable workaround for an employer's policy: check what is permitted before making an offer.

Agree what recording and quotation permissions cover, who can access the material and how it will be retained. The GOV.UK guidance on [research consent](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/getting-users-consent-for-research) is a practical process reference; adapt the arrangement to your organisation and study.

## Use a recruitment tracker that exposes gaps

For each candidate, record only what the study needs:

- Participant ID, source channel and contact permission.
- Relevant event, role and recency evidence.
- Eligibility decision and reason, including unresolved questions.
- Invitation, screening, booking and attendance status.
- Agreed compensation and fulfilment status, where applicable.
- Any permission or access issue that must be resolved before interviewing.

Review the pattern of exclusions as well as completed sessions. If only one role or one customer cohort responds, decide whether to add a channel, revise an unnecessary filter or narrow the claims the study can support.

## Decide how much evidence the study needs

There is no universal “B2B interviews per segment” rule. The question's breadth, variation among participants, analytic approach and consequences of a wrong decision all affect the evidence needed. An exploratory interview study does not become statistically representative because it reaches a fixed count.

Plan an initial round, review what is unresolved and reserve capacity for additional perspectives. Record the rationale in the [research plan](/blog/user-research-plan-template/). Avoid claims that every B2B audience reaches saturation faster than a consumer audience.

## Review the process before the next study

Compare invitation, screening, attendance and usable-session counts by channel, with their actual denominators. These are operational measurements for this recruitment effort; they are not universal benchmarks. Keep changes to the screener visible so later analysis can account for who entered under which criteria.

Maintain a reusable panel only with an appropriate basis for future contact. The [research operations guide](/blog/research-operations/) connects recruitment ownership, consent and evidence reuse across studies.
